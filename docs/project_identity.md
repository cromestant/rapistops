# RapistOps — Project Identity

**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Started:** 09/30/2026

## Why RapistOps Exists
Sexual violence can leave information scattered across survivors, reports, investigations, institutions, courts, prosecutors, public records, and other sources.

A report may exist without a conviction.

An investigation may exist without prosecution.

A case may be dismissed.

Multiple reports involving the same person may exist in different places without being connected.

Records can also change, disappear from public view, or remain difficult to discover without knowing exactly where to look.

RapistOps exists to make this fragmented information easier to find, preserve, connect, and understand.

The goal is accountability through information: making documented records harder to lose, isolate, or obscure.

## Who RapistOps Is For
RapistOps is ultimately concerned with accountability for survivors and victims of sexual violence.

The system is intended to support people who need to understand documented information about sexual violence, cases, institutions, and relationships between records.

Survivors are not required to prove that a criminal conviction exists for their experience to be represented as a documented report or record.

At the same time, RapistOps must distinguish a survivor's report or allegation from facts that have been independently established or adjudicated.

## The Problem
Information about sexual violence does not exist in one system.

Relevant information may be distributed across:
- Survivor reports
- Police departments
- Investigations
- Prosecutors and district attorneys
- Courts
- Schools and educational institutions
- Employers and organizations
- Public records
- Public documents
- Public registries
- Other lawfully accessible sources

These records may use different terminology, formats, identifiers, and systems.

They may describe different parts of the same event or case.

They may also document different outcomes.

RapistOps is intended to provide infrastructure for connecting these records while preserving the identity, provenance, context, and status of each source.

## Accountability Without Conviction-Only Thinking
RapistOps does not define accountability as equivalent to conviction.

The system should be capable of representing the existence of:
- Reports
- Allegations
- Investigations
- Charges
- Proceedings
- Dismissals
- Acquittals
- Convictions
- Other documented outcomes

These states must remain distinguishable.

A report is a fact about the existence of a report.

A conviction is a fact about a legal outcome.

Neither should automatically be transformed into the other.

The absence of a conviction should not cause a documented report or proceeding to disappear from the system.

## What Makes RapistOps Different
RapistOps is not intended to duplicate survivor-controlled documentation and confidential matching systems.

Systems such as Project Callisto focus on giving survivors a private mechanism for documenting experiences and, where appropriate, identifying potential matches with other survivors.

RapistOps has a different primary focus.

RapistOps is intended to connect fragmented information across lawfully accessible sources and institutions.

Its core problem is:
    **How can documented information that exists in different places be preserved, connected, searched, and understood as part of a larger record?**

This includes relationships between:
- People
- Reports
- Evidence
- Cases
- Events
- Institutions
- Sources
- Outcomes
- Time
- Locations

## Core Principles

### Survivor-Centered Accountability
The purpose of the system is ultimately connected to accountability for survivors and victims of sexual violence.

### Preserve the Record
The existence of a report or documented proceeding should not disappear simply because it did not result in conviction.

### Preserve Context
Information should retain its source, date, jurisdiction, status, and surrounding context.

### Preserve Provenance
The system should make it possible to determine where information came from and when it was collected.

### Distinguish Claims From Established Facts
Reports and allegations must not automatically be represented as proven facts.

Documented legal outcomes must not be misrepresented as something else.

### Connect Fragmented Information
Related records should be capable of being connected through explicit relationships rather than remaining isolated.

### Preserve History
Changes to records should be trackable over time rather than silently replacing previous information.

### Minimize Harm
The system should not become a tool for harassment, threats, stalking, doxxing, retaliation, or unnecessary exposure of survivors or other people.

### Use Lawful Information
RapistOps should operate using publicly available or otherwise lawfully accessible information and respect applicable access restrictions.

## The Long-Term Vision
The long-term vision for RapistOps is a system in which documented information about sexual violence does not have to remain scattered across disconnected sources.

A search for a person, case, institution, event, or record should be capable of revealing its documented relationships to other relevant information.

The system should make it possible to examine:
- What was reported?
- Where was it reported?
- What records exist?
- Which institutions were involved?
- What happened afterward?
- What documented outcomes occurred?
- What relationships connect these records?
- What changed over time?

The purpose is not to manufacture conclusions.

The purpose is to make the available record more connected, traceable, and understandable.

## Version 0.1.0
Version 0.1.0 is the first working foundation of this larger vision.

It will establish the core ability to:

**collect → preserve → structure → connect → store → search → display**

The first version is intentionally limited.

It establishes the foundation from which the larger RapistOps system can eventually grow.
